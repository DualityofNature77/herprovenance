Her legacy

We come from women who shaped nations.

From the Blue Mountains of Jamaica to the Ashanti Kingdom, from Mississippi cotton fields to the halls of Congress: Black women have freed people, organized movements and defended what was sacred. Their record is ours to carry.

Winning women

Know what’s best about you.

Not winning at the expense of each other. Winning together: with standards for how we treat one another, with spirit as strategy, and with the receipts for our own work.

Know your worth

Excellence is inherited. Name your gifts out loud and price your work accordingly.

Lift as we climb

How we treat each other is the movement: credit each other, share the room, refuse the tear-down.

Spirit as strategy

Our foremothers prayed, sang and planned in the same breath. Rest, ritual and alignment are part of the work, not a break from it.

Keep the receipts

Your ideas, your hours, your results, dated and documented. The woman with the record is hardest to erase.

The Record · in development

Keep your name on your work.

Ida B. Wells understood it first: the record protects us. The Record is a private app that time-stamps your ideas and files the moment you make them, logs what happens next, and exports a dated timeline for HR, a lawyer or the EEOC. Your files never leave your device.
